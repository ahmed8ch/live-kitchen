Restaurant Wi-Fi vs. 4G/5G Reality in Egypt

1. The Quota Abuse Problem: In Egypt, commercial and home internet packages are quota-capped (e.g. 140GB, 250GB, 300GB per month). If a restaurant gives open Wi-Fi passwords, guests often stream YouTube/TikTok or download heavy files, consuming the restaurant's entire monthly quota in days.

2. How Real Restaurants Solve It:
• MicroTik Captive Portal Bandwidth Throttling: Speed is capped at 1.5 Mbps per device (enough for viewing the menu and WhatsApp, but chokes downloads).
• Walled Garden (Whitelisting): Only the ordering domain (matbakhlink.com) is unthrottled and free. Streaming sites are blocked.
• Time / Quota Limit: 60 minutes or 75MB per device MAC address.

3. Mobile Data Dominance: In practice, 85-90% of diners in Egyptian cafes and restaurants use their own 4G/5G data bundle because scanning a table QR code on an iPhone or Android directly launches Safari/Chrome over cellular data without fiddling with Wi-Fi passwords.

Conclusion: MatbakhLink is engineered to be featherweight (under 150KB gzip) so it loads in <500ms on 4G cellular data, while providing guest Wi-Fi details on the physical stand as a backup for poor indoor reception.
